Regarding the speed and strength of action — there are several points.

1). Increasing the DOSE does not always increase the effect; for example, with herbs that speed up the pulse, increasing the dose can replace a sedative effect with a stimulating one. Increasing the dose of toxic herbs can replace a sedative–psychedelic effect with toxic effects (which can produce stimulation). For example, a 5mm×5mm square of dried belladonna leaves gives a mild blissful–sedative effect until the middle of the next day (as an ointment), but a 6mm×6mm square gives NO sedative effect at all, because it causes arrhythmia and a feeling of poisoning.

2). I am not without reason immensely impressed by those Italian witches. Their mixture "water-parsnip / cinquefoil / bittersweet nightshade / iris" gives an ideal result: after an hour and a half the effects of water-parsnip and cinquefoil begin; after 4 hours they fade, but after 7 hours the effects of nightshade and iris turn on (and last several more hours)! The components are chosen PERFECTLY. The necessary doses of both water-parsnip and nightshade produce noticeable toxic effects (from water-parsnip in the first hours — discomfort in the epigastric area, and by morning a metallic taste in the mouth; from nightshade — noticeable nausea), but IRIS completely REMOVES these effects, allowing the doses of these herbs to be raised safely! Iris itself also gives a noticeable relaxing–psychedelic effect by morning. So water-parsnip with cinquefoil take you "into the Astral" in the first hours; nightshade with iris — by morning. This mixture also had another component: bat blood. It was added to COMPENSATE for the blood-thickening effect of nightshade (it reduces blood clotting, acting as a thrombolytic). I replaced it with ASPIRIN. Without it, after an hour and a half nightshade causes a HEADACHE — the first sign of blood thickening (and this is dangerous). With aspirin this effect is completely absent.

3). As an ointment the effect is smoother and longer, stretched over time, because the ointment acts like a "slow drip" (which is exactly what you want). Eating it makes the onset of effects sharper, but the end of the action comes faster (because you ate it all at once and there is no gradual "prolongation" from a "slow drip" feeding this chemistry into the blood all night). In addition, if you apply it not to the wrists (where the vessels are) but to a part of the body far from vessels, you can get an even smoother prolonged effect (and the dose can be increased, because stretching the effects over time reduces their intensity).

4). In the second recipe of those witches (with thapsia) SOOT was added to the ointment (candle soot; you can blacken an object and scrape it off). This was also GENIUS: soot is YOUR IDEA WITH ACTIVATED CHARCOAL!! You can look it up: SOOT as an ADSORBENT is significantly better than activated charcoal. For oral intake soot would be pointless, but as a component of an ointment — it is a brilliant find!

YES, these mixtures are not strong enough for "a nurse to say 'I'm giving you an injection' — and in that very moment the soul would fly up to the ceiling." Even the "cottony" body from a sore throat is much more "cottony" than from nightshade with irises. So the result "leaves something to be desired," but the result IS there: these mixtures really help me go into the Astral. And this is certainly incomparable with, for example, Castaneda's recipes (which turn a person "inside out" from intoxication). The Italians even learned to completely REMOVE mild nausea with iris. That is, they did not chase the result at any cost, but arranged ideal "comfort" for themselves (unlike the Indians): their mixtures are COMPLETELY free of unpleasant effects!

In my reconstruction of their recipes, the role of ASPARKAM (in the morning after "flights") remains unclear: without it things can really go badly. Almost all of those witches' herbs deplete potassium and magnesium. WHAT did the witches use to replenish potassium and magnesium? Yes, in Italy the sea is nearby — seawater would be a good substitute for Asparkam; at least it would replenish magnesium perfectly. Potassium — you could dissolve ash from a fire in water. BUT what exactly those witches used (and whether they used anything) — most likely, this will remain an unanswered question.
