# Preface {#predislovie}

While doing experiments with broad-leaved water-parsnip and bittersweet nightshade, I acquired Asparkam and magnesium sulfate. For the experiment, I decided to try to obtain magnesium citrate by an exchange reaction of magnesium sulfate with calcium citrate. Calcium sulfate, in principle, is poorly soluble and may precipitate (it forms crystal hydrates, which seem to be soluble after all). For this I was going to take chalk or eggshell, but I decided to go further and took a street stone for this.

**ATTENTION! OBTAINING MAGNESIUM CITRATE FOR INTERNAL USE IS RISKY BECAUSE THERE MAY BE IMPURITIES**

Here is this stone:

![foundstone](/experimets/AceMg/foundstone.jpg)

I was looking for limestone or calcite, but finding it in the city on the way home was difficult; however, near a store I saw fragments of white stones. It looked like one that contains calcium, albeit dirty.
I decided to take it.

The reaction proceeds slowly. Probably low contact area. When the reaction is almost finished, it can be assumed that we have at most ~13 g of calcium acetate.
Then for the exchange reaction of calcium acetate and magnesium sulfate we need quite a lot of grams of magnesium sulfate. According to rough calculations, 10 grams.

I took magnesium sulfate:

![reaction.gif](/experimets/AceMg/reaction1.gif)
